What this does and does not tell you


This tells you how hard the ground shakes. It does not tell you how to build a
house that survives it. Those are different problems, and the second one kills far more people.


Whether a house stands up is decided by much plainer things: a continuous tie beam at the top of
the walls, reinforced corners, enough wall length in both directions, and a roof actually fastened
to the walls. If that is what you need, these people already do it properly and for free:


    
  
  • Build Change — safe self-built and retrofitted housing

This is not engineering advice. It is a teaching model: one uniform layer, linear
elastic soil, vertically travelling shear waves. Real ground is layered, gets softer as it is shaken
harder, and can liquefy. Basin edges and hillsides add amplification this ignores entirely. Nothing
here is a site-specific evaluation or a substitute for a licensed engineer where you live.
